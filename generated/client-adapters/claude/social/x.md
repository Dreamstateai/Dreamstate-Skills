# X

Depth for X drafting, research and publishing. Read `hooks.md` first for the psychological triggers behind every hook rule below.

## The 280-character rule is absolute

Count the drafted body before proposing or saving a single-post draft. X shows several competing posts on screen at once, so the hook carries even more weight here than on other platforms: assume near-zero attention budget. When content does not fit in 280 characters, either cut it down or thread it explicitly, with every part numbered (`1/`, `2/`, ...) and each part independently within the limit. Never propose an over-length single post, and never silently truncate one to make it fit.

## Hook construction (first line, or first tweet of a thread)

Specificity beats vagueness: an exact number ("7 strategies", "$847") reads as credible, a round or vague one ("some strategies", "nearly $1,000") reads as uncertain. Stack triggers when possible rather than relying on one: a curiosity gap plus a contrarian angle outperforms either alone. Five reusable hook shapes: shocking stat then contrarian insight then a numbered promise; exclusivity ("X% don't know this, here are the best N for [year]"); before/after ("[time] ago I struggled with X, now I [result], here's what I'd tell myself"); myth-buster ("the path to X isn't what you think, it's actually Y"); analysis ("I looked at [large N] of these, here's what stood out"). Replace feature language with a concrete outcome: rewrite "AI-powered" or "10x faster" into the specific life or work outcome it produces, by asking "so what" of the feature until it lands on an emotion.

## Thread structure

Number every tweet in a thread. Each one should stand alone as valuable, not lean on the previous one for justification. The hook tweet carries disproportionate weight for whether the rest gets read at all. Post the full thread within about 2 minutes of the first tweet; a delayed drip breaks the read-through chain. End with an explicit ask (follow, reply, share) rather than implying one. Reserve threads for weekdays; keep weekend posts to single tweets.

## Engagement mechanics: two separate signals

Distribution into a wider feed is driven mainly by reposts/quotes: a post with zero reposts effectively never leaves its starting audience regardless of likes. Ranking, once a post is already circulating, is driven mainly by reply chains, especially chains where the original author replies back. Treat these as two separate levers when planning a follow-up or engagement pass, not one blended "engagement" concept. Hashtags no longer help reach and can hurt it, except one genuinely tied to a live trending event.

## Content pillars and funnel mix

Personal, Expertise (how-to and listicle formats are the strongest performers here), Social proof, Trending (have a take ready within hours of a trend breaking, and skip it if it does not genuinely fit), Opinions (only ones actually held; never fabricate a stance for engagement). Shift the top-of-funnel/middle/bottom mix with account maturity: a new or small account should stay mostly top-of-funnel and reach-oriented; do not front-load bottom-of-funnel asks onto an audience that does not know the account yet.

## Research: recent window vs full archive

X research runs through the official API source. Recent search covers at most the last seven days. Full-archive search is available only when the connected project's entitlement explicitly grants it; do not infer archive access from the presence of older cached rows in canonical tables, since cached history is not proof of live entitlement. State the search window used and the entitlement state plainly rather than presenting recent-only results as if they were exhaustive.

## Metric truthfulness

Preserve post id, author identity, source scope, observed time and rights policy alongside every research row. Likes, replies, reposts and quotes are commonly available; impressions and bookmarks can be entitlement-gated or provider-dependent. Missing is never the same as zero. A generic web search can surface an X URL but cannot supply structured, provider-verified metrics for it; do not present a web-discovered number as a measured one.

## Brand voice and scoring before saving

X voice is punchy and opinionated, but the opinion must be one the account actually holds. Prefer one clear claim, concrete language, and a first line that can stand alone in the feed. Match learned cadence, casing, punctuation, humor, and vocabulary before applying hook formulas. A neutral source can support an assertive synthesis, but it cannot justify inventing certainty, outrage, or personal experience. Use the social voice profile before product-document voice, and disclose the fallback when no current social voice exists.

Check brand voice freshness before drafting from scratch for a connected account; a `stale` or `no_data` state means there is no learned style to match and that should be disclosed. Score a draft before saving when scoring is reachable, and disclose a `heuristic_only` fallback level or low confidence rather than presenting the estimate as strong.
