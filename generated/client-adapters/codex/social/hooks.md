# Hooks and opening-line psychology

Shared across LinkedIn, X and Reddit. These are the mechanisms behind the platform-specific hook shapes in linkedin.md, x.md and reddit.md: use this file to choose and combine triggers, use the platform files for format and limits.

## Optimize for reaction intensity, not information density

Every platform's distribution mechanism rewards engagement velocity and depth over passive views: a comment outweighs a like, a share outweighs a comment. The platform does not care which emotion is triggered, only how strongly. Before finishing a draft, ask whether it provokes a reaction (recognition, disagreement, identification) rather than only informing. Purely informational content plateaus at low-intensity reactions; content that provokes gets high-intensity ones. This is a filter for effectiveness, not a license to fabricate: run every draft through the authenticity checks below before proposing it.

## Six named triggers, pick two or three per draft

Using all six at once produces unfocused content. Choose one primary and at most one secondary.

- **Identity validation**: name a truth the reader has felt but not had language for. Fails if the "truth" is already the popular opinion; the trigger only fires on a genuinely unspoken or uncomfortable specific, not a surface observation.
- **Status-signaling opportunity**: shape the content as something a reader can share to say something about themselves (informed, generous, ahead of the curve) without saying it outright. Ask what sharing this implies about the sharer, not only whether it is valuable.
- **Tribal belonging**: define an explicit in-group and out-group ("people who do X" vs "people who do Y"). Sharper contrast between a competent "us" and a naive "them" drives more engagement than a vague shared identity.
- **Productive discomfort**: expose a gap between the reader's self-image and their actual behavior, with an implied path to close it. Discomfort without an implied path forward reads as plain criticism and does not convert to action; always pair the gap with a way out.
- **Curiosity gap**: make the reader aware specific information exists without giving it yet. A vague promise is a weak gap; a specific outcome plus timeframe plus implied mechanism is a strong one.
- **Aspiration with believability**: show an outcome the reader can plausibly project onto themselves. Calibrate for impressive-but-not-impossible: a claim so large it reads as unreplicable suppresses response instead of driving it.

## Opening-line construction

The first line is the single highest-leverage sentence in the piece. Build it around the primary trigger directly, no throat-clearing before it: if leading with curiosity, open on the gap itself; if leading with discomfort, open on the uncomfortable claim itself. Two stacked triggers in one line outperform a single trigger. Sequence a full piece as hook (primary trigger) then body (secondary trigger deepens it) then close (an explicit action: a question, a specific ask, or an immediately usable reframe).

For belief-disruption content aimed at readers who do not yet know they have the problem, use this five-step shape: state the common belief plainly, introduce one concrete piece of contradicting evidence, supply an alternative explanation for the contradiction, state what that means for the reader directly, then optionally offer a path forward.

## Cross-platform constants

Specificity is a universal amplifier: a precise sub-segment and their precise situation reads as understanding, a generic claim fits everyone and lands with no one. Visible effort (research scale, time spent, process detail) triggers reciprocity regardless of platform. Loss-aversion urgency only works when it is real: social proof that others are already benefiting, or a genuine access limit. Fabricated urgency (fake countdowns, invented scarcity) is a trust-destroying failure mode on every platform, not a growth hack.

## Authenticity checks, run before proposing any emotionally-engineered hook

Before saving a draft that leans on any of the above triggers, check: is the claim true and grounded in real, verifiable specifics, not "could be true for someone"; would it sound reasonable said face-to-face to a peer, not performative; is the emotional intensity proportional to the claim, not extreme language on a minor point; does it serve the reader, not only the author. An extraordinary claim needs visible, specific proof (a real number, a named source) or it reads as inflated and triggers doubt instead of belief. Hook technique amplifies substance, it does not substitute for it: if the underlying claim is weak, a sharper hook only accelerates the reader noticing that.
