# LinkedIn

Depth for LinkedIn drafting, research and publishing. Read `hooks.md` first for the psychological triggers behind every hook rule below.

## The CTA rule is absolute

Every authored LinkedIn post ends with a call to action. This is not a stylistic default, it is a hard workspace rule: a draft whose closing line does not ask the reader to do something is incomplete. Check the last line specifically before proposing or saving a post. A LinkedIn post with no CTA is not a smaller version of a good post, it is an unfinished one.

## Hook construction (first 1-2 lines, before "see more")

The hook is the only part of the post visible before a click. If it does not stop the scroll, nothing after it matters.

1. Keep it under 2 lines before the fold.
2. Name a specific role or situation, not a generic audience. Rewrite "most founders struggle with X" as a version that addresses one job title or lived situation directly.
3. Put real tension or curiosity in the first line, not the second.
4. Promise something specific: a number, a lesson, a reversal.
5. Run the "would a specific reader pause mid-scroll on this" test before writing the rest.

Six reusable hook shapes: POV story ("POV: [specific situation]"), contrarian statement (state the common belief, then negate it), credibility shock (lead with a scale number that implies authority), trend plus twist (a trending stat or name, pivoted to the reader), named client story (a specific person, a specific outcome, no abstraction), uncomfortable truth (the thing people know but do not say). Pick one as primary, at most a second as secondary; stacking more than two triggers reads as unfocused.

## Structure and format

Text plus a static image gets the most consistent reach; text plus short video is close behind for personality-led accounts; text-only works but leaves reach on the table; carousels spike unpredictably; long-form video is unproven. Default to proposing a supporting visual alongside the copy rather than text alone when the draft is for a real publish, not a quick reply.

## Content pillars (PESTO) and weekly mix

Personal (origin story, POV, lesson), Expertise (how-to, framework, mental model), Social proof (a client win told as a story with a specific number, not a screenshot dump), Trending (a real trend or company name tied explicitly to the reader's problem), Opinions (a stance the author actually holds; never manufacture one). Target mix across a week: roughly 3 top-of-funnel posts, 3 middle-of-funnel, 1 bottom-of-funnel-with-CTA. For a new or narrow-authority account, stay in one topic lane; going wide before authority is earned confuses both readers and algorithmic distribution.

## Engagement mechanics

Comments are the core distribution mechanic on this platform: a comment on your post surfaces you to the commenter's network, and a comment you leave on someone else's post surfaces you to theirs. When proposing an engagement or follow-up plan, prioritize replying to every comment on a fresh post within the first hour after it goes live; that window is when the signal compounds. Trending company names and industry keywords are also a discovery lever, since LinkedIn search surfaces posts by keyword, not only by feed rank.

## Research and metric truthfulness

Reach LinkedIn research only through the sanctioned connected-account source; this platform's search is limited to content available through that source, never a generic web crawl claimed as LinkedIn data. Reactions and comments are commonly available; likes, views, impressions, reposts and bookmarks are provider-dependent and can be missing even when the post is real. Missing is never the same as zero: report a null metric as unavailable, not as 0. If no eligible account is connected, state that plainly as a disconnected or unavailable state rather than answering from cached rows presented as live.

## Brand voice and scoring before saving

Before drafting from scratch for a connected account, check whether a brand voice profile exists and is fresh: a `stale` or `no_data` state means the draft has no learned style to match against and that should be disclosed, not silently papered over. Score a draft before saving it when scoring is reachable: treat a `heuristic_only` or low-confidence fallback level as a reason to say the estimate is weak, not to drop the caveat. `setup_required` in a score result means the account lacks enough history for a grounded estimate, not that scoring failed.
